## Symbol Timing Recovery Questions

**1. Show that in the absence of noise, α and φ in Eq. (2) have no impact on the maximum output energy solution.**

After matched filtering, with no noise,

```
y(nT + τ) = α e^{jφ} sqrt(Ex) Σ_m s[m] g((n-m)T + τ - τd)
```

With zero-mean, uncorrelated symbols of unit energy, `E|s[m]|^2 = 1`, so

```
J(τ) = E|y(nT + τ)|^2 = |α e^{jφ}|^2 · Ex · Σ_m |g(mT + τ - τd)|^2
     = α^2 · Ex · Σ_m |g(mT + τ - τd)|^2          (since |e^{jφ}| = 1)
```

- φ drops out entirely because `|e^{jφ}| = 1`.
- α only scales J(τ) by the positive constant `α^2`.
- A positive scale factor does not move the argmax: `τ̂ = argmax_τ J(τ)` is still the τ where `τ - τd` is a multiple of T, i.e. `τ̂ = τd` (mod T). So α and φ have no effect on the max-energy solution.

**2. What are the two critical assumptions used to formulate the indirect maximization of the output energy? How can flat fading / AWGN impact the method, and how might you mitigate this with parameter selection?**

Assumptions (from the derivation in Sec. 1.4):

1. **Expectation and derivative can be interchanged**, and the expectation can be replaced by a **time average over P symbols**: `d/dτ E|y|^2 ≈ (1/P) Σ_n d/dτ |y(nT+τ)|^2`.
2. **The derivative can be approximated by a first-order (central) difference**: `d/dτ y*(nT+τ) ≈ y*(nT+τ+δ) - y*(nT+τ-δ)`, which only holds if δ is small relative to how fast the pulse varies (i.e. the signal is heavily oversampled).

Impact of the channel:

- **AWGN** adds a random term to every sample, so the time-averaged statistic `J_δ[k]` is a noisy estimate. The difference `r[k+δ] - r[k-δ]` with a tiny δ is a small quantity, so noise can dominate it and the zero crossing / peak gets mis-located.
- **Flat fading** (attenuation α) scales the useful part of `J_δ[k]` by `α^2` while noise is unchanged, which lowers the effective SNR of the statistic (the phase φ cancels in `Re{r · conj(...)}`; see Q1).

Mitigation via parameters:

- **Increase P** (average over more symbols) — assumption 1: the time average then approaches the true expectation and the noise averages out. Longer packets help.
- **Choose δ and M sensibly** — assumption 2: large oversampling M keeps a small δ (e.g. δ = 1) accurate. If noise is the problem, a larger δ (several samples) gives a larger difference signal relative to noise, at the cost of a coarser derivative approximation. Pick the largest δ for which the first-order difference approximation still holds.

**3. After downsampling a sequence originally sampled at rate 1/Tz by a factor M, what is the sample period of the resulting signal?**

The new sample period is **M · Tz**. Since `Tz = T/M` (Fig. 1), this equals the symbol period **T**.

## Timing Error Statistic ε(N)

Settings: τd = 0.34 Ts, h = 0.75e^{jπ/4} (single channel tap), noise power = −∞ dB, QPSK, 500-bit packets, RC filter (α = 0.5, length 8), 200 iterations per run. N = RX oversample factor, N = 2, 4, …, 20.

Ts was held constant across the sweep by scaling the RX sample rate with N. Only even decimation factors are available at low sample rates, so the sweep used Ts = 7.2 µs (TX oversample 4, TX sample rate 0.5556M, delay 2.448 µs; RX sample rate = N × 0.13889M). N = 14 has no valid sample-rate pair at 7.2 µs, so it used Ts = 7.28 µs (TX 0.5495M, RX 1.9231M, delay 2.475 µs). ε is normalized by Ts, so the point is comparable.

**Observations**

- **Trend:** ε falls as N grows for both algorithms, because the estimator can only choose one of N sample phases per symbol. The curve is jagged, not smooth, because the best achievable error depends on how close 0.34 Ts is to a multiple of 1/N. N = 6, 12 and 18 sit almost exactly on a phase (2/6 = 0.333), so Max Energy reaches about 4e-5 there.
- **Max Energy vs Early-Late Gate:** Max Energy is lower than Early-Late Gate at almost every N (the exception is N = 4, where the two are within about 3%). Early-Late Gate uses a finite-difference derivative of a noisy average, so it is more sensitive to the random data.
- **N = 2 for Early-Late Gate:** ε = 0.1156 = 0.34², i.e. it estimated zero delay. With two samples per symbol and a one-sample difference step (half a symbol), the first-order derivative approximation does not hold. The manual's remark that these algorithms need heavy oversampling applies.
- **Run-to-run variation:** results are not deterministic even with noise at −∞ dB; the data bits are random, and the expected-energy peak is flat near its maximum at large N, so a neighbouring phase is sometimes chosen. N = 12 for Max Energy gave 0.002120 on one run and 0.000044 on another. The statistic is not a stable average over the 200 iterations, so we report the median of repeated runs where available.
- **Not tested:** the effect of AWGN and of packet length was not measured here. **[GAP — needs experimental run]**

## Programming

- `adityam_align_MaxEnergy.vi` — built and verified in the simulator. Sum of |r|² over each of the M sample phases, k̂ = index of the maximum, waveform rotated left by k̂, `offset` = k̂. A shared subVI `adityam_phase_sum.vi` reshapes the array into rows of M and sums each column.
- `adityam_align_ELgate.vi` — built and verified in the simulator. `J_δ[k]` (δ = 1) from the neighbour-rotated array, summed per phase with `adityam_phase_sum.vi`; k̂ is the index where `J_δ` crosses from ≥ 0 to < 0 (the manual's "max/min" wording is inconsistent, and the zero crossing is the energy peak).
